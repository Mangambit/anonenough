## Notes on why this email is written the way it is

- **The ask is in the first sentence.** Organizers on deadline day are triaging a full inbox;
  a request buried in paragraph three gets skimmed past.
- **One line of apology, then stop.** Over-apologising reads as negotiating and invites a no.
  "No excuse" closes the topic faster than an explanation would.
- **The commit history is the argument.** Their actual concern isn't punctuality, it's
  fairness — whether you had extra building time. Timestamped public commits answer that
  directly, which is a much stronger position than asking for a favour.
- **You make it easy to say yes.** Links work, tests run, nothing for them to chase.
- **You give them a graceful no.** Someone who's clearly fine either way is easier to say yes
  to than someone whose disappointment you'd have to manage.

**Don't:** invent a technical excuse, mention a time zone mix-up you didn't have, or send it
from a "we" voice — you're a solo participant and that's a strength here, not a weakness.
